A bell began to jingle as the night train moved through the mountain tunnel. The sound was faint, almost cheerful, but it warned the crew that something had come loose. A passenger trampled a paper cup underfoot and rushed toward the window. No one wanted to fret, but the train had stopped between two long slopes.

The locomotive began to tilt as snow accumulated on one side of the track. The engineer tried to revolve the wheels slowly, hoping to find traction. A deflector plate had shifted and could repay them with a dangerous derailment if they ignored it. To compensate for the lost speed, they needed a careful plan.

The railway company could not default on its duty to passengers. The crew used a hoe to clear the inlet beside the rails and began to infer the shape of the blockage from marks in the snow. A passenger tried to retort that the danger was exaggerated. The conductor did not scoff. She asked everyone to broaden the search for blankets and water and to beware of stepping onto unstable ice.

The train had two cars full of students returning from a conference. Their chatter filled the dark carriage. A small stove began to decompose the last dry fuel, producing more smoke than heat. A passenger tried to grab the emergency door, but the conductor urged him to wait. Another traveler gave a quiet shrug and helped distribute food.

The company had to supervise every movement while the storm worsened. They tried to concentrate on one task at a time. If the mountain became completely blocked, they would terminate the journey and evacuate. A farmer offered to transplant the passengers to a nearby barn, where warm straw and a diesel heater waited.

A railway worker carried a hand-cranked xerox machine to copy the passenger list. It seemed absurd until the power failed completely. A horse began to trot beside the train, and a passenger scrambled to open a vent. The train would have to trot forward slowly, but only after the track was solid.

A maintenance crew arrived with a tow vehicle. They worked clockwise around the damaged coupling and used a counter lever to shift the jammed plate. The scene could have become a slaughter if a wagon had slid down the slope, so no one hurried. The situation was melancholy, but not hopeless. The team was calm.

At dawn, the train moved eastward. A Muslim family in the last carriage shared tea with a group of hikers. The snow had buried the road, yet the rails remained visible. The crew brought the passengers into the next station without injury. Their success was not spectacular. It was the result of patience, clear instruction, and a refusal to panic when the night seemed endless.

The journey gave every passenger a thrill, but the crew refused to let excitement become a riot. They had to entreat the crowd to remain calm and obey the conductor. A bird began to peck at a bag of grain near the window. The snow was starting to solidify around the wheels.

The passengers considered a boycott of the railway after the delay, but the manager asked them to listen first. Someone had tried to plunder the emergency supplies during the night. The crew made one final endeavor to move the train and deflect the wind with a temporary barrier. A loose tarp began to flap against the roof.

The engineer rejected a headlong rush toward the next station. Among the passengers was a Moslem family who had been traveling to visit relatives. They shared blankets with strangers and helped distribute food. The train reached the platform hours late, but no one was missing. The crew accepted the delay as the price of a safe journey and the passengers understood why.

The railway learned that a safe arrival can be a greater thrill than a fast one.
